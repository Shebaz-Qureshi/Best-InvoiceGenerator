Product and Wholesale Invoicing: Shipping Charges, Volume Tiers, and SKU Itemization

A product invoice has one job at the buyer's end: to match what was ordered, what shipped, and what is owed. Clear itemization makes that quick.

Selling physical goods to retailers, distributors, or corporate buyers is more complicated to invoice than selling services. Instead of one or two lines, you may have dozens of products, different unit prices, volume breaks, freight charges, and a purchase order that has to match. The buyer's team will compare your invoice against the order they placed and the delivery they received, line by line.

When those three documents agree, payment flows. When they do not, the invoice waits in a queue. This guide covers how to itemize products clearly, show discounts and shipping correctly, and reduce the mismatches that delay wholesale payments.

What buyers check on a product invoice

  • Purchase order match: does the PO number and each line agree with the order?
  • Delivery match: do quantities agree with what arrived?
  • Price match: do unit prices agree with the quote or price list?
  • Charges: are shipping, discounts, and taxes shown and explained?
  • Payment details: are terms and instructions clear?

Itemizing products clearly

Use SKUs and part numbers

Descriptions vary, but a SKU identifies exactly one product. Include the code next to a short description so both warehouse and accounts staff can match the line quickly.

State quantity and unit

Say whether you mean single units, packs of 12, or pallets. Show the price per unit and the extended total for each line.

Keep lines consistent

Use the same order and format across every invoice so buyers who process many documents know where to look.

The wholesale billing cycle

  1. Receive the purchase order and record its number.
  2. Check stock and pricing against your price list.
  3. Calculate discounts and freight for the order.
  4. Ship and invoice, referencing the PO and tracking details.
  5. Send a PDF invoice to the buyer's accounts payable contact.

Volume discounts and price tiers

Volume pricing rewards larger orders, but it can confuse buyers if it is not explained. There are two common ways to show it.

Tiered unit prices

The unit price drops when the quantity crosses a threshold, for example 4.50 per unit for 100 to 199 units and 4.20 for 200 or more. State the tier that applies on the line.

A separate discount line

Keep list prices on each line and subtract a clearly labeled discount at the end. This is easier to audit, because the buyer sees both the price list and the reduction.

Shipping and handling

Show shipping on its own line instead of hiding it inside unit prices. Buyers often need to allocate freight separately, and tax treatment can differ from the goods. Include the carrier, service level, and tracking reference in the notes where possible.

  • Separate shipping from handling if you charge both.
  • State whether shipping is prepaid or collect.
  • Confirm whether freight is taxable in your jurisdiction.
  • Reference the packing slip or shipment number.

Freight terms and international shipments

For palletized or cross-border orders, state the agreed Incoterm, such as EXW, FOB, or DDP, in the notes. It clarifies who arranges transport, who bears risk in transit, and who pays duties and taxes. Include the currency, the buyer's import or tax number where required, and a clear description of goods for customs. Requirements differ by destination, so confirm them with your freight forwarder or a customs adviser.

Matching purchase orders and packing slips

Large buyers often use three-way matching: the invoice must agree with the purchase order and the goods receipt. Mismatches in price, quantity, or product code send the invoice to an exception queue. To avoid that:

  • Put the buyer's PO number on the invoice in the reference field.
  • Invoice only what shipped, not what was ordered but backordered.
  • Use the same product codes as the buyer's order.
  • Send a separate invoice for backordered items when they ship.

Payment terms for product orders

Wholesale terms are often longer than service terms, commonly Net 30 or more, and are measured from the delivery date or the invoice date. Be explicit about which. New buyers may need to pay upfront or in part until a payment history builds. For established accounts, consider an early-payment discount if faster cash is valuable to you, and state any late fee in the agreement before the first order ships. Clear terms on the invoice remove the guesswork for the buyer's accounts team.

Preparing invoices for dropshipping and marketplaces

When a third party fulfills the order, several addresses and references are in play. Keep the billing party, the delivery recipient, and the order number clearly separated. Include the marketplace or channel order ID, the fulfillment partner, and the tracking reference where relevant, so any question can be traced to a specific shipment.

Common product-invoicing mistakes

  • Missing PO numbers, which delay approval
  • Invoicing backordered items before they ship
  • Burying freight inside unit prices
  • Unexplained discounts that do not match the price list
  • Inconsistent product names between order and invoice

Returns, credits, and corrections

If goods are returned or an item was wrong, issue a credit note that references the original invoice instead of editing it. State the reason, the items, and the amount credited. This keeps both sides' records aligned and makes the account easy to reconcile.

Frequently asked questions

Should shipping be charged tax on a product invoice?

It depends on the country or state and on how shipping is treated for the goods sold. In some places delivery is taxable when the goods are, and in others it is not. Confirm the rule for your location.

Why list SKUs on an invoice?

SKUs let the buyer's warehouse and accounts team match each line to the goods received and to their purchase order. Without them, matching depends on descriptions, which are easy to misread.

How do I show a volume discount?

Show it as a separate labeled line or as a clear percentage applied to the merchandise subtotal, so the original price and the reduction are both visible.

What are Incoterms and do I need them?

Incoterms are standard trade terms, such as EXW, FOB, or DDP, that say who arranges shipping and pays costs and duties at each stage. For international or freight shipments, stating the agreed term on the invoice avoids disputes.

Can I invoice for dropshipped orders?

Yes. Use the buyer's billing address for the invoice and the recipient's address for delivery, and reference the order number so the transaction is traceable.

When should I send a wholesale invoice: at order or at shipment?

This follows your agreement. Many sellers invoice at shipment, others at order confirmation with a deposit. State the trigger and the payment terms clearly so the buyer knows when payment is due.
